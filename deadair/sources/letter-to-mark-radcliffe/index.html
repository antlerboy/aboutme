Letter to Mark Radcliffe

RADC.DOC · Ben Taylor · saved 22 September 1996

The postal and email addresses in the heading, the exact birthday, and the pager number are omitted. Spelling and wording follow the file. Read or save the plain-text letter.

Dear Mark,

I'm writing to say thanks for a lot of enjoyment over the years. I first listened to 'Out On Blue Six' when I regularly missed the second-last train back from Manchester to Glossop, the 21:55, and had to wait for the last at 23:20. At that time I was strapping my alto saxophone to the back of my bike, cycling to Glossop station, then from Piccadilly down to Salford Lads Club for Monday night rehearsals with The Jesus Burgers. This was a hard punk/funk/rock group (FNM/Chilli Peppers in their dreams) into which my unique alto sax style didn't really fit. But it was alright, 'cos no-one could hear me anyway. And we were usually hampered by not having a guitarist. So we used to climb out on to the roof and look out over Manchester, and the other guys would tease me for being a sad spotty music obsessed virgin from Glossop. Whereas of course they were sad spotty heay metal-obsessed virgins from Salford. So on a snowy night after the pedals had fallen off my bike while going through the Ordsall Estate it was somehow comforting to listen on my walkman to three songs on the theme of vegetables played by a sad... well, no need to get personal. Anyway, what has happened to those dead clever 'three songs loosely connected to the same word' features? By the way, the Jesus Burgers only played one gig while I was in the band. And I was on holiday at the time.

Fast forward a bit to when I spent 6 months as a volunteer looking after a paralysed bloke in Birmingham. However difficult things seemed, I always knew that I could settle down with a 4-pack of 'Tiger Lager' (Aldi's own brand, 99p, 1.2% alcohol) and listen to... well, three songs about vegetables, or something. But with a reassuring accent. And the genuinely useful Bedsit Survival Guide. Anyway, things perked up a bit when I got a Danish girlfriend (and began to wish I'd listened to aforesaid survival guide a bit more closely), and the spots cleared up a bit.
Forward again and I'm sitting in a VW camper van on holiday in London with my dad and our kid. It's my 19th birthday, [date omitted], and me brother has just given me 'I, Jonathan' by Jonathan Richman. We are listening to your show, and Jonathan Richman is on! Shame he was such a grumpy bastard. By the way, 'Lesbian Bar' has pride of place on Joe's superb 'Blue 6' compilation tape (I'll get him to send you a copy - if Nicky Campbell can sell mix CDs I reckon you should market it!) and the version on 'I, Jonathan' is better than the version on 'Surrender to Jonathan'.

So thanks for all that and much more. You can't be a DJ for long without affecting people but John Peel says his reason for DJing (apart from the money, drugs and so on) is that he hopes one day to play exactly the right song at the right time for someone. Well, you've done that many times for me, like when I was driving home from a gig by my mate's band The Birth of the True and you played 'Pillar to Post'.

Incidentally, you can get a publication called 'Big Carp Magazine' (but it costs £3.99 so get your own), the currency for the Tameside L.E.T.S. scheme is the Bobbins, and I once saw in a catalogue 'Henry the Hedgehog Welly Tree', a convenient and attractive place to store up to four pairs of wellies, a snip at £49.99.

Anyway, first of all I reckon you should play a tune for me. You can choose one of these but don't just play any old tune like you usually do.
- 'The Electrician' by the Walker Brothers
- 'I Was Dancing in the Lesbian Bar'
- anything by Aztec Camera, especially 'Boy Wonders'
- 'This Is The Day' (Naff Organ Version) - The The

Secondly, Oxford Student Radio (Oxygen 107.9fm - Right Off the Top of the Dial!) has been granted a full regional license from next February, and I very much hope to be presenting a show. It will have live music, poetry, my treasured collection of charity shop records ('The Happy Hammond Goes Pop Country', 'John Inman and a Bit of Brass, featuring the Massed Voices of the West Mercia Police Choir and the Webb Ivory Brass Band'), some good music too and the theme will probably be 'Popcorn' (probably the Phantom Surfers' version). And, er, it might very well 'feature' three songs about vegetables. I might even start with 'New Rose' too. So, any chance of an interview? Record a few 'idents'? Give us a few tips? (What was that third song about vegetables?) I'd be even more grateful. Maybe you could E-mail me if you might be able to do something, or page me on [pager number omitted].

Anyway, thanks for all the tunes and for extending my musical obsession and supply of drug-addled trainspot trivia, and remember - in 5 years, when you get sacked from the BBC and go back to ILR, if I notice an article in the paper about it, I'll say 'Mark Radcliffe! I remember him! Wasn't he a sidekick to that Marc Riley bloke?' Best wishes to Marc and his family.

Yours,
Ben Taylor
